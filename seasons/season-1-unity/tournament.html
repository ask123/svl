2 · Serve, toss & line-up

Game-day toss & line-up

One coin toss before the match — and we make it a little more interesting. 😁 Team Owner + one team member attend the toss.

Step 1

Toss winner chooses

The toss winner picks either Serve first or preferred court side. The other team gets the remaining option.

Step 2

Toss loser declares first

The toss loser declares their 7th & 8th players first.

Step 3

Toss winner declares

The toss winner then declares their 7th & 8th players.

Step 4

Line-ups recorded

Both line-ups are written down before the match starts.

Step 5

7th & 8th enter after 8 points

They can come on once either team reaches 8 points. Later substitutions follow the team’s strategy and rotation rules.

🔀

Serve order across sets (Semi & Final)

Toss applies to Set 1: choose serve → you serve first; choose court → the other team serves first. Set 2 starts with the opposite team serving. If a Set 3 is needed, whoever served first in Set 1 serves first again.

🚫

No libero this season

No libero is used in VPL Season 1. All 8 players follow normal rotation and substitution rules. After 8 points, any player can replace any other regardless of court position; before 8 points, no substitutions.

5 · Fair playing opportunity

The fair-play rotation

This is the heart of VPL. It ensures every player gets a reasonable chance to play — while owners still make their own tactical decisions.

Each team has 8 players. Every league match, 6 start and 2 sit as the 7th & 8th — marked S in the tracker. The S players can enter once either team reaches 8 points. A starter cannot be substituted before the 8-point mark; after it, substitutions follow the owner’s strategy.

How it works

Match 1: Players 1 & 2 are S; Players 3–8 start; 1 & 2 can enter after 8 points. Match 2: Players 3 & 4 are S; the rest start. And so on across all 8 matches — so every player is S twice, starts 6 matches, and gets the first 8 points in 6 matches. After 8 points, the owner can make further substitutions by strategy.

This is only an example — the same two players don’t have to be S together, and owners can change the combinations to suit their team.

Availability & injuries

Follow the rotation where reasonably possible. If a player is injured, unavailable, unable to continue, or has another genuine reason, the owner may adjust the rotation. The committee takes genuine circumstances into account. Communicate openly within your team and manage it fairly.

7 · Fairness safeguard

Formal player complaint

The rotation rule provides a fair opportunity — it is not meant to interfere with normal team strategy. Try to resolve concerns within the team first.

If a player feels they didn’t get a reasonable opportunity

They may submit a formal complaint to the VPL Organising Committee, which may review participation, the S-rotation record, availability, injuries, team circumstances and any other relevant information.

If the complaint is upheld:

  • The player must start the semi-final.
  • If the team reaches the Grand Final, the player must start that too.
  • The player cannot be substituted in those matches unless injured or unavailable.
  • The Team Owner must refund the player’s $50 registration fee.

This is a fairness safeguard, not a tool to control normal team strategy.

3 · Serving rules

Serving

🔄

Rotation is mandatory

No player may serve continuously — serve rotation must be followed.

🦶

Behind the end line

Serve from behind the end line until after contact with the ball. Underhand or overhand is allowed, and the ball must be tossed before serving.

🥅

Net serves play on

A served ball may touch the net and continue into the opponent’s court. It must land inside the court.

🙅

Serve can’t be attacked

A serve cannot be attacked or blocked — it must be received with a pass. Maximum 3 touches per side.

4 · Playing the ball

Playing the ball & faults

3️⃣

Three touches

Max 3 touches to return the ball. A block doesn’t count as one of the 3. No consecutive double-hit, except as permitted after a block.

〰️

Off the net & the lines

The ball may be played off the net during a rally. A ball touching a boundary line is in.

✋

Legal contact

Contact with any body part without catching, carrying, palming or throwing. Simultaneous contact by two players counts as one team touch.

🧱

Net contact

Touching the net while the ball is in play is a fault — except when the ball is driven into the net hard enough that the net then touches an opponent; play continues.

🙌

Reaching over the net

Only allowed on a natural follow-through, or when legally blocking an attack, and without interfering with the opponent’s play.

➖

Centre line

Don’t completely cross the centre line if it interferes with an opponent or creates an advantage. Player safety comes first.

Full list of basic violations (faults)
  • Stepping on or over the end line while serving.
  • Failing to serve the ball over the net.
  • Carrying, palming or throwing the ball.
  • Illegal contact with the ball.
  • Touching the net with any part of the body while the ball is in play.
  • Illegally reaching over the net.
  • Reaching underneath the net in a way that interferes with an opponent or the ball.
  • Completely crossing the centre line with the foot in a way that interferes with an opponent or creates an advantage.
12–15 · Conduct & safety

Refereeing, responsibilities & safety

🧑‍⚖️

Refereeing

The referee’s decision during a match is final. Teams take turns providing umpiring support. Respect decisions; don’t delay the match. Unresolved issues go to the committee.

👔

Team Owner responsibilities

Manage the squad, plan rotation, keep S rotation fair, handle availability/injuries, make sure players know the rules, support umpiring, and set the tone for good behaviour.

🤝

Sportsmanship

VPL is a community tournament. Respect teammates, opponents and referees; accept decisions; play fairly; avoid arguments and aggressive behaviour; prioritise safety; make it positive for everyone.

⛑️

Player safety & insurance

Players participate at their own risk and are responsible for their own insurance and medical needs. Tell your owner if injured; don’t continue if it’s unsafe. Organisers aren’t liable for injuries, accidents or losses.
