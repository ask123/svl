The idea

Why an auction, not a regular league

Most weeks, you play with the same seven people. Mixer Cup exists to break that up — one competition where every regular player from every team gets shuffled into a fresh line-up and plays against people they usually only warm up next to.

The auction is what makes it fair and interesting: instead of friends picking friends, five teams are assembled competitively, so the talent — and the fun — is spread evenly across the season.

What comes out of draft night is five genuinely new teams, playing a proper double round robin to find out who's best when the usual lines are gone.

Season path

From sign-up to final whistle

Five stages, in order. Nothing about the schedule is decided until the teams are.

Step 1

Registration

Players register individually and pay the $50 entry fee. Everyone goes into the same pool — no pre-formed teams.

Step 2

Auction night

Five team captains draft their rosters from the full player pool. Each team ends up with 8 players.

Step 3

Schedule released

Once the five teams are finalized, the full weekday match schedule (7–9 PM slots) goes out to everyone.

Step 4

Double round robin

Each of the 5 teams plays every other team twice — 20 league matches in total, one set each.

Step 5

Winner & runner-up

Top two teams from the round robin table take the season — $500 for first, $300 for second.

On court

How matches are actually played

The format is built to guarantee everyone gets real game time — and to keep matches moving.

25

Scoring format

Every league match is a single set, played to 25 points.

If the set reaches 24–24 (deuce), play continues — but the deuce win-by-2 rule is capped: whichever team reaches 30 points first wins outright, regardless of margin.

8

Squad size & game time

Each team has 8 registered players for the season.

Every player on the roster must play a minimum of 3 matches across the season — captains have to rotate the squad, not just run the same six.

Fair playing-time rule
6+2

Match-day squad

Before each match, the team captain names a starting six and two substitutes from the 8-player roster.

Only those 8 are eligible to play that match.

!

No early substitutions

No substitution is permitted during the first 10 points of the set — including for injury.

If a player is unable to continue in that window, the match continues with the team playing short-handed. Substitutions are only allowed once the set has passed 10 combined points.

Applies even in case of injury
